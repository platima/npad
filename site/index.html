The Notepad you remember, minus the bloat.

npad is a tiny native text editor that opens in about 40 milliseconds and behaves like Windows 10 Notepad. It quietly adds what Notepad never had: crash recovery, saves that can’t destroy your file, dark mode and print preview.

Familiar on day one. Better every day after.

npad follows one rule: out of the box it behaves like Windows 10 Notepad. Extras that can’t hurt you are on. Anything that changes how Notepad works is off until you ask for it.

Your work is safe, even from you.

  • Crash recovery snapshots on your first edit and every 30 seconds after. Snapshots never touch your file.
  • Atomic saves write a temporary file, verify it, then swap it in. A failed save can’t destroy the original.
  • Encodings and line endings are detected and preserved, and a warning appears before an ANSI save would lose characters.

How saving works →

  1. Write your text to a temporary file
  2. Verify what was written
  3. Rename it over the original
  4. If anything fails, your file is untouched

Dark when you want it. Notepad-light when you don’t.

Choose light, dark, follow the system, or Solarized Light and Dark. Changes apply live to every open window, including the title bar, scroll bars and drop-down menus. Even the app icon can follow your setting.

And the rest of what you’d expect

Everything Notepad does, plus the small things it should have done years ago.

Find & Replace

Direction, match case, whole word, wrap-around, a live match count and optional highlight-all.

Print & preview

Page Setup, header and footer codes, and a Print Preview with paging, two-page view and zoom that wraps exactly where the page does.

Per-window zoom and fonts

Ctrl+Scroll to zoom, Ctrl+M for monospace. Separate monospace and proportional fonts, with an OpenDyslexic option.

Unicode throughout

UTF-8, UTF-8 BOM, UTF-16 LE/BE and ANSI are detected and kept as they were.

Open it your way

Drag and drop, Ctrl+Drop to insert, a recent files menu, npad file.txt from any terminal, and “Open with” for any file.

Undo that goes deep

A 100,000-step undo and redo history, so you can back out of almost anything.

What npad will never be

Simplicity is the feature. These are deliberately left out.

  • No AI
  • No spellcheck
  • No syntax highlighting
  • No rich text
  • No frameworks
  • No forced or silent updates
  • No background services

Questions

Is npad free?

Yes. npad is open source under the MIT licence. If it saves you time you can sponsor the project, but you never have to.

Will it replace Windows Notepad?

It installs alongside. The installer can optionally register file types and a notepad alias for Win+R, and npad appears in “Open with” for every file type. It never takes over a file type behind your back, and you can change associations later in Preferences.

Does it send any data anywhere?

No. There is no telemetry and no background service. The only network request npad can make is an update check, and that is off until you turn it on.

Why does SmartScreen warn about the download?

Releases are code-signed, but SmartScreen builds a publisher’s reputation over time. Right-click the file, choose Properties, tick Unblock and Apply. Details.

What about tabs, spellcheck or syntax highlighting?

Spellcheck, syntax highlighting and AI are deliberately out of scope. An optional tabbed interface is on the roadmap.

Where are my settings?

In a plain JSON file: %APPDATA%\Platima\npad\settings.json on Windows and ~/.config/npad/settings.json on Linux. Preferences can also export and import them.
